{{% theorem name="" index="" status="" %}}

Let $f: (a-r, a+r) \to \R $ and $g: (a-r, a+r) \to \R $ be functions analytic
on $(a-r, a+r) $, with power series expansions

$$f(x) = \sum\_{ n=0 }^{ \infty } c\_n(x-a)^n $$

and

$$g(x) = \sum\_{ n=0 }^{ \infty }d\_n(x-a)^n $$

respectively.

Then $fg: (a-r, a+r) \to \R $ is also analytic on (a-r, a+r), with power series
expansion

$$f(x)g(x) = \sum\_{ n=0 }^{ \infty }e\_n(x-a)^n $$

where $e\_n = \sum\_{ m=0 }^{ n }c\_md\_{n-m} $

The sequence $(e\_n)^ \infty\_{n=0} $ is referred to as the **convolution** of 
the sequences $(c\_n)^ \infty\_{n=0} $ and $(d\_n)^ \infty\_{n=0} $.

{{% /theorem %}}
